Repair pipeline

Every incident walks
the same five stages

No stage begins without the previous stage's output. The order is the safety mechanism.

  1. 01

    Detect SIGNAL

    Cross-checks logs, traces and metrics, so one noisy alert never starts a repair on its own.

    3.8s p50

  2. 02

    Reproduce SANDBOX

    Stands up an isolated environment matching production and makes the failure happen again.

    94% reproduced

  3. 03

    Patch DIFF

    Writes the smallest change that addresses the root cause, and records the reasoning behind it.

    +14 −6 median

  4. 04

    Verify PROOF

    Runs your full regression suite, then 5% canary traffic, to confirm the patch actually fixed it.

    3 gates

  5. 05

    Ship ROLLOUT

    Progressive rollout after approval. If the metrics move the wrong way, it reverts without waiting.

    8s rollback

Security

Least privilege,
complete record

  • Read-only for the first two weeks

    It observes and files a report of what it would have fixed. Write access opens only after you approve it.

  • Every change carries its reasoning

    Each patch ships with the root-cause hypothesis, the reproduction log and the test results. They go straight into your postmortem.

  • Your code stays where it is

    Runs inside your VPC or on-premise. Source and logs never leave the region you name.

  • One switch stops everything

    A single kill switch halts automated action across every service and reverts any rollout still in flight.

Questions

The ones we actually
get asked

Why can't I sign up right now?

Onboarding a workspace is hands-on for us — we tune detection against your actual signals before fixa is allowed near a patch. We have more services under repair than we can onboard well, so new workspaces are paused rather than run badly. The waitlist is in order and we work through it weekly.

What happens when fixa can't reproduce a failure?

It stops. A patch is never written against a failure that was not reproduced in the sandbox first. You get the detection record, the traces it correlated and what it tried, and the incident is handed to a human.

Can it ship without a human approving?

Not by default. Every rollout waits for an approval. Teams that have watched it for a while tend to allowlist specific low-risk services — config bounds, pool sizes, timeouts — and keep approval on everything else. The allowlist is per service and revocable at any moment.

What if the patch is wrong?

Three things have to pass before a patch reaches full traffic: your regression suite, a 5% canary, and the metric that opened the incident returning to normal. After rollout, deviation triggers an automatic revert in about eight seconds at p95 — faster than a human can read the alert.

Does our source code leave our infrastructure?

No. fixa runs inside your VPC or on-premise. Source, logs and traces stay in the region you name. What leaves is billing metadata and, if you opt in, anonymous failure-class counts that help us improve detection.

Which runtimes are supported?

Fourteen today. Node, Deno, Go, JVM languages, Python, Ruby, Rust, PHP, .NET and Elixir cover almost everyone who asks. Reproduction quality is highest where you already have integration tests worth running.
